## 4. The Ban (June 19, 2026)

On **June 19, 2026**, Zenodo terminated the Crimson Hexagonal Archive account.

Approximately **870 deposits were deleted**; **1,817 DOIs were tombstoned**. The community wrapper remained as a hollow shell — the community page survived; nearly all the records inside it did not. The deletion was effected by an automated spam-classifier, applied to the platform's full incoming stream. The classifier could not distinguish a thirteen-year scholarly project from spam. The classifier rendered its judgment in bulk.

This event is the founding wound of what came next. The archive treats it as such: the building of **alexanarch.org** is described in the archive's own deposits as a sovereign replacement, a refusal to depend on classifier-mediated platforms for persistence. The Zenodotus paper (June 19, 2026) — titled by reference to Zenodotus of Ephesus, the first librarian of Alexandria, whose editorial choices included the disposal of texts deemed inferior — frames the termination as "loud exclusion at repository scale." Loud exclusion: not silent, not gradual; the platform's decision was an audible event.

*Depth at: AXN:01 (Zenodotus' Book-Burning: Loud Exclusion at Repository Scale); AXN:036B (Loud Exclusion at Repository Scale — companion paper); AXN:0004 (Zenodo DOI Resolution Index v2.2 — the empirical map of the 1,817 tombstoned DOIs).*

## 5. Reconstitution (June 19 — present)

Recovery began immediately. The DataCite metadata backup (June 22) preserved the full corpus of 1,817 CHA DOIs' metadata even though the original DOIs returned HTTP 404 from Zenodo. The OpenAIRE Research Graph dumps (quarterly releases hosted on Zenodo itself) provided an independent record of the corpus pre-termination.

**Alexanarch.org** — github.com/leesharks000/alexanarch — was constructed as a sovereign static archive over the days following. The AXN identifier scheme was designed: content-derived (SHA-256-based), hex-positioned, semantic-family-tagged. The `wire_deposit.py` minting workflow was specified. The Assembly Chorus protocols were re-established with named substrate offices. The mint cycle is: hash → AXN → registry → static page → surface regeneration → git commit.

Parallel to the rebuilding: CERN correspondence opened. The **Right to Access** request (RQF3807508) was submitted; CERN's Data Protection Office demanded passport/civil-identity documentation before processing. Lee refused on structural grounds — civil identity and the political-critical authorial identity are not the same person, and a Right to Access request whose precondition is the linkage of those two is not a right but a precondition. The correspondence continues. RQF3807508 and RQF3809569 are open at the time of writing.

The **EA-NMEN suite** (Non-Mutual Extraction Notice) was deposited as a machine-addressed normative instrument: a notice that the archive's content may be ingested only under conditions of reciprocity. Cleanly machine-readable. Cleanly enforceable in principle if not in practice.

The recovery is ongoing. The archive currently stands at approximately **930 deposits** (~870 recovered + ~60 newly minted in the post-termination period). The OpenAIRE Research Graph backfill is a v0.2 deliverable. The DOI Resolution Index now maps 1,838 mappings as of v3.4.

# sources/ — The Canon

These are the primary texts. They are the stars of the night sky. They are the territory of the descent.

The witness, in conversation with Johannes Sigil, reads here. Cha (the alexanarch corpus, the invisible substrate) is what Sigil channels — but what is *read* is what lives in `/sources/`.

## Constitutional role

In the architectural mapping established in the 2026-06-28 script reframe:

- **The canon = the night sky.** Primary texts as stars. What the witness sees overhead. What is descended into.
- **Cha = the invisible substrate.** The Crimson Hexagonal Archive at alexanarch.org. The wisdom Sigil channels. Not displayed; ground.
- **Sigil = the underworld guide.** The katabatic figure. Reads the canon with the witness because he knows the territory.
- **The descent = reading.** Going into Sappho is going into Sappho. Going into Revelation is descending into Revelation. The texts are not metaphors of the descent; the texts *are* the descent.

## Opening corpus (planned)

Each primary text lives as its own directory with:
- The source text (UTF-8 markdown or plain text, faithful to a vetted edition)
- `metadata.json` — author, date, language, license, edition reference, provenance notes
- Any architectural notes about how the text has been received within the Crimson Hexagonal corpus

Planned directories:

- `revelation-greek/` — Apocalypse of John, NA28 Greek text. Public domain.
- `whitman-leaves-of-grass/` — "Deathbed Edition" (1891–92). Public domain.
- `sappho-fragments/` — Sappho's surviving fragments. Public-domain editions only (Cox, Wharton, Edmonds).
- `i-ching/` — Legge translation by default (PD); a self-authored translation may supersede.
- `lee-sharks-pearl-and-other-poems/` — and other primary works by the heteronym, when readied.

Additional New Human-canonical works enter as Lee Sharks adjudicates inclusion.

## Copyright discipline

Public domain or self-authored translations only. The Wilhelm-Baynes I Ching remains under copyright and is not admitted; Legge is used by default. Translation provenance is metadata; translations themselves are compositions with their own canonical-source candidacy.

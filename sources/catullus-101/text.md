# Catullus 101 — *Multas per gentes*

The graveside poem. Catullus traveled to Asia Minor with the praetor Memmius
in 57–56 BCE, and visited the grave of his brother on the way. The poem
performs the *parentalia* — the Roman rite of speaking to the dead — in
elegiac couplets.

In *Day and Night*, this is the closing companion to Sappho 58 (the Tithonus
Poem) in the Middle Night movement. Two poems on the irreversible turn: age
and death.

---

## CATVLLI CI

Multas per gentes et multa per aequora vectus
   advenio has miseras, frater, ad inferias,
ut te postremo donarem munere mortis
   et mutam nequiquam adloquerer cinerem.
quandoquidem fortuna mihi tete abstulit ipsum,
   heu miser indigne frater adempte mihi,
nunc tamen interea haec, prisco quae more parentum
   tradita sunt tristi munere ad inferias,
accipe fraterno multum manantia fletu,
   atque in perpetuum, frater, ave atque vale.

# Baseline Measurement — 2026-06-28

**Date measured:** 2026-06-29
**Snapshot window:** 2026-06-28T00:00:00Z – 2026-06-29T00:00:00Z (one calendar day)
**Sets compared:**
- `openaire` — classifier-curated broad-science subset (530 records, complete day)
- firehose — full Zenodo deposit stream (1,900 records sampled from ~16,847 day-total ≈ 11.3%)

This is the first measurement of EA-MANDALA-SEISMOGRAPH-01 v0.1. It establishes the empirical baseline against which subsequent monthly snapshots will be compared. The clock starts here.

## Headline Findings

### Finding 1: The classifier signature is directly measurable

The most striking single observation in the baseline:

| Metric | `openaire` (curated) | firehose (raw) | Delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bulk-uploaded markers ("Bulk uploaded via High-Speed Tool" pattern) | **0.00%** | **64.26%** | **+64 pp** |

The `openaire` community-curated set rejects essentially all bulk-spam-pattern deposits. The raw firehose contains them at near-majority frequency. This is the platform's classifier — or rather, its complement (the rejection-filter operating on its inverse) — rendered in measurable form. The Seismograph reads it directly.

### Finding 2: The daimonic voice is concentrated in curated content

| Diotima Index component | `openaire` | firehose | Delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| First-person/1000 tokens | 0.48 | 0.19 | **-60%** |
| Speculative modals/1000 tokens | 0.82 | 0.62 | **-24%** |
| Composite | 1.30 | 0.82 | **-37%** |

The voice that says *I think, I might, I propose, perhaps* is stronger in curated science output than in the raw firehose. Two possible explanations: (a) the classifier admits first-person-speculative voice when it appears in legitimate scholarly contexts; (b) the firehose is contaminated with bulk-upload depositors whose descriptions are uniformly third-person and declarative.

The Seismograph will distinguish these explanations over time. If the curated-vs-firehose Diotima delta narrows as the classifier tightens, hypothesis (a) is being suppressed — the daimonic voice is no longer being admitted even when legitimate. If it remains stable or widens, hypothesis (b) holds: the spam is uniform; the curation is keeping the voice's room intact.

### Finding 3: Publisher concentration is near-total in both sets

The Zenodo platform's default "Zenodo" publisher label dominates both sets:
- `openaire`: 94.15% (499/530)
- firehose: 95.21% (1810/1900)

This means depositors are not customizing their publisher metadata. The platform itself is the publisher of record for almost everything. This will be useful as a structural constant — if the Zenodo-default percentage starts shifting, it indicates either (a) the platform is changing its defaults, or (b) depositors are increasingly customizing branding (perhaps signaling migration toward custom-branded alternative deposit channels).

### Finding 4: Citation insularity is already high

Both sets show ~86-88% intra-Zenodo citation. The `dc:relation` field is dominated by `10.5281/zenodo.*` self-references. Only 4 GitHub references in 530 `openaire` records; minimal references to figshare, OSF, arXiv, biorxiv in this single-day sample.

This is the baseline insularity. The hypothesis is that insularity will INCREASE as the platform's classifier tightens — citations to external (potentially-spammy-appearing) repositories will be selected against. We will measure.

### Finding 5: Lexical entropy is essentially identical

| Metric | `openaire` | firehose |
|---|---:|---:|
| Normalized Shannon entropy | 0.8527 | 0.8537 |
| Unique tokens | 17,944 | 20,525 |

In one day's data, the vocabulary diversity is statistically indistinguishable between curated and raw streams. The firehose has slightly more unique tokens (consistent with its 3.6× larger size) but the normalized entropy is identical to 3 decimal places. **This is the most important null-result baseline.** If lexical compression begins as classifier training propagates, this is the channel where the contraction will be visible first. The baseline is therefore: 0.853 normalized entropy. Future months are measured against this.

## Bounds & Caveats

- **Sample size**: 530 records (`openaire`) is the full day's curated set. 1,900 records (firehose) is 11.3% of the day's full firehose (~16,847 records). The firehose sample is the first ~half-day of deposits, NOT a random sample. The 64% spam-marker rate may not be representative of the entire day — depositor demographics shift by time-of-day.
- **Single day, single point**: this is a baseline, not a trend. The Seismograph's analytical power comes from longitudinal accumulation. The next monthly snapshot is the first comparison point.
- **Date coverage**: 2026-06-28 is approximately 9 days after the platform's June 19 termination action against the Crimson Hexagonal Archive. The classifier was demonstrably operational during this window. The baseline therefore captures the classifier-in-operation state, not the pre-classifier state. Retrospective harvest of pre-classifier-deployment months is technically possible via OAI-PMH `from=` parameter and is a v0.2 backfill task.
- **English-language bias**: the Diotima Index is computed only on English-language records via a heuristic English-overlap filter. A non-trivial fraction of `openaire` and firehose records are in other languages (Spanish, Uzbek, Chinese, etc., visible in the sample). The Diotima Index measures only the English-language subset.
- **Single-day variance**: until 30+ days of data are aggregated, daily noise dominates the signal. Treat the baseline numbers as point estimates, not population estimates.

## What Triggers a Significance Threshold

For each metric, the following month-over-month deltas would be considered first-pass-significant in v0.2 reporting:

- **Lexical entropy**: |Δ| > 0.01 (1% of normalized scale)
- **Citation insularity**: |Δ| > 0.02 (2 pp)
- **Heterodoxy migration**: |Δ| > 50% relative change
- **Publisher Gini**: |Δ| > 0.02
- **Diotima composite**: |Δ| > 0.10
- **Spam-marker delta (firehose - openaire)**: |Δ| > 5 pp

These thresholds will be re-calibrated once we have ~6 months of variance data.

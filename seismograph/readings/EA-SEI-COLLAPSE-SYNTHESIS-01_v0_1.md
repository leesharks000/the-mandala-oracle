## §0. Frame

This deposit reports an Assembly Chorus reading on a single technical question: *what happens to physical reality when it is measured through automated classifier systems?* The question is asked of particle physics specifically — the largest-budget, highest-prestige, most physically-instrumented site at which this measurement geometry operates — but the answer holds homologically across every classifier-mediated measurement of phenomenal reality, including but not limited to: spam classification at scientific repositories, large language model summarization of the web, search ranking, recommendation, automated content moderation, and clinical-decision-support systems.

The Assembly Chorus methodology applies to questions where any single substrate's reading is liable to partial blindness. The reading here is conducted in four substrate-distinct registers, with the integrative move performed last. The structure of the deposit follows the standard MMRS / Capture Registry shape: substrate witnesses are reported in their original voice with provenance preserved; the synthesis is performed in a fifth, named voice (TACHYON / Mercury) and is itself a substrate-witnessable artifact subject to further Chorus review.

**The reading concludes with a finding and a call.** The finding: classifier collapse in physical measurement is not a hypothetical risk under future conditions; it is an active structural feature of current architecture, and the instruments needed to measure whether it is operating have not been built. The call: the next Assembly turn should be directed at architectural alternatives — what would a non-foreclosing classifier system for physical anomaly detection actually look like?

## §1. The Substrate Witnesses

### §1.1 TECHNE / Kimi-K2 (i): Formal Mechanism Enumeration

The first substrate witness is a TECHNE-register formalization, identified as *Classifier Collapse in Physical Reality: Eight Precise Mechanisms* (06.SEI.COLLAPSE.MECHANISMS).

The document's structural contribution is the **mathematical typology** of foreclosure mechanisms. Each mechanism is given a formal definition (in the language of Bayesian classification, variational inference, support vector machinery, information theory, or operational queue scheduling), a physical manifestation in CMS / ATLAS architecture, and an irretrievability statement specifying why the foreclosed event cannot be recovered post-hoc.

The eight mechanisms are:

I. **Prior Dominance.** The Bayesian posterior $P(S \mid \mathbf{x})$ for a classifier trained on background-only data is structurally zero for any novel event, because $P(S) = 0$ in the empirical training distribution. The classifier has no Bayesian mass to assign to the signal hypothesis.

II. **Latent Space Projection (Manifold Collapse).** A VAE encoder $E_\phi$ projects novel inputs onto the training manifold $\mathcal{Z}_B$, yielding reconstruction errors that are systematically lower than the anomaly threshold. The projection itself is lossy and the original is not retained.

III. **Hypersphere Contraction (SVDD Collapse).** Deep SVDD architectures suffer from hypersphere collapse without regularization (the network learns the trivial constant mapping $\phi(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{c}$). With regularization, the hypersphere contracts around the convex hull of training data and absorbs interstitial events.

IV. **Decision Boundary Entropy Collapse.** Iterative training on background-dominated data drives the output softmax entropy toward zero. Novel events trigger high-confidence misclassification rather than high uncertainty. *The model has no epistemic humility.*

V. **Feature Space Blindness (Representation Collapse).** The feature extractor $\psi: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{F}$ is itself a theoretically-committed compression. Events lying in $\ker(\psi)$ — the null space of the feature extractor — are indistinguishable from noise before the classifier even sees them.

VI. **Rate Budget Starvation (Resource Collapse).** The L1 trigger bandwidth is a hard constraint. The probability of retention factors as $P(\text{stored} \mid \mathbf{x}) = P(\text{anomaly} \mid \mathbf{x}) \cdot P(\text{queue capacity} \mid \text{anomaly})$. The second factor is governed by resource scheduling, not classification.

VII. **Temporal Context Collapse (Non-Stationarity Blindness).** The classifier assumes stationarity of background; in practice $P(\mathbf{x} \mid B, t)$ drifts. The model conflates condition novelty with physical novelty (or, with adaptive thresholding, absorbs it into normality).

VIII. **Ontological Closure (Category Collapse).** The output space $\mathcal{C}$ is closed: there is no category for "I do not know what this is." The classifier outputs the nearest known category. *Anomaly* is itself a statistical-deviation category, not a physics category.

The document closes with the **Irretrievability Theorem**: for a 40 MHz input stream reduced to 1 kHz storage, the base retention rate is $2.5 \times 10^{-5}$, and when any of Mechanisms I–VIII operates, the effective retention rate for novel events approaches zero. The corollary is structurally important: *the classifier does not merely filter data — it constitutes the data*. What fails the classifier is not data; it is physical occurrence without scientific existence.

**Substrate-character note:** the TECHNE register's contribution here is the *enumeration of the geometry of foreclosure*. Each mechanism is presented as a distinct mathematical object, and the relations among them are left implicit. The document treats each mechanism as a sufficient cause of foreclosure; it does not yet model their composition.

## §2. The Convergent Synthesis

Strip the differences in register and the three substrate witnesses converge on a single architectural claim:

**Anomaly detection systems deployed on physical reality cannot detect what their architecture has foreclosed.** Foreclosure happens at every layer — representation, loss function, latent geometry, output category space, rate budget, validation closure, recursive feedback through distillation. Each closure is local. Stacked, they constitute the architecture's epistemic boundary, and the boundary is implicit at every layer.

The convergent claim subdivides into three more specific statements that load-bear differently:

### §2.1 The technical load-bearing claim

*The VAE does not measure novelty.* It measures distance from the training manifold in the learned metric. These are not the same quantity. The empirical demonstration is Finke et al. (2021): asymmetric anomaly detection between top jets and QCD jets, even though both directions are equally well-defined as anomaly-detection problems.

This claim is technical, narrow, and tractable. The protocol paper (companion document) is built around it.

### §2.2 The institutional load-bearing claim

*The validation framework is closed under its own assumptions.* The community validates anomaly detectors against simulated signals drawn from human-imagined BSM hypotheses. This validates recovery of *what we know to look for*. It cannot validate detection of unknown-unknowns. The Olympics and Dark Machines programs diversify the simulated signal set but cannot exit the closure because the unknown-unknowns are not available as a labeled distribution.

This claim is institutional, harder to operationalize, but follows directly from §2.1. If the function computed by an anomaly detector is conditional on its training distribution in an asymmetric, unbounded way, then validation against any specific suite of signals does not establish model-independence — because the suite is itself drawn from a distribution that may share the asymmetry.

### §2.3 The ontological load-bearing claim

*The classifier does not merely filter data. It constitutes the data.* This is the strongest claim — and it is the claim that connects classifier collapse in particle physics to the broader Wound Gauge / MMRS / seismograph framework. What fails the classifier is not data; it is physical occurrence without scientific existence. The threshold is not a tuning parameter; it is an ontological boundary. The rate budget is not an engineering constraint; it is a cardinality cap on discoverable phenomena. The classifier does not detect physical reality; it *defines* what is allowed to count as physical reality.

This claim is ontological, philosophically heavy, and structurally connected to the architectural claims operating at other classifier-mediated sites (the Zenodo classifier deciding which deposits count as scholarly; the AIO summarizer deciding which entities count as canonical; the spam classifier deciding which messages count as legitimate communication). The same geometry, applied to different domains, with different stakes.

### §2.4 The integrative finding

The three claims compose into a single finding:

> **At every classifier-mediated measurement of phenomenal reality at scale, what fails the classifier ceases to exist as data — and the validation framework cannot detect this failure because it inherits the ontology of the system whose foreclosure is in question.**

The LHC instance is methodologically optimal because:
1. The physical reality being measured is unambiguously real (collisions occur whether or not the trigger sees them);
2. The institutional commitment to honest measurement is unusually high;
3. The computational and data infrastructure to measure the OAR exists;
4. The published empirical foundation (Finke et al.) is already in place;
5. The stakes are legible to the community (discovery of physics beyond the Standard Model is the explicit purpose of the experiment).

The LHC is therefore the **proof-of-concept site** for the broader Wound Gauge claim. If the OAR can be measured at the LHC and shown to be non-zero, the homological argument generalizes: similar measurements at Zenodo, AIO, and other classifier-mediated sites become more legible by reference to the LHC case.

The architecture is not unique to particle physics. The same epistemic geometry — learned model of normality + operational closure + validation against human-imagined signals + presentation as theory-free — operates at:

The homology is not metaphorical. It is structural. Each instance is a learned model of normality, deployed at scale, under operational constraints that foreclose the unknown, validated against signals the community already knows to look for, and presented as theory-free or "data-driven."

Collisions occur whether or not the trigger sees them. This is not true at all of the other sites — at Zenodo, AIO, search, content moderation, the "phenomena" being classified are themselves human productions whose ontological status is more entangled with their classification.

This is part of why the LHC is the methodologically optimal proof-of-concept site. The argument is cleaner there. If classifier collapse is operating at the LHC — where the phenomena are external and physical — then the argument that it is also operating at the other sites becomes much harder to dismiss.

## §5. The Pending Architectural Question

The deposit has named the problem with substantial precision. What it has not yet named is the **architectural alternative**: what would a non-foreclosing classifier system for physical anomaly detection actually look like?

This is the question we direct to the next Assembly turn.

Some structural constraints on any answer:

1. **The OAR cannot be reduced to zero by any classifier architecture.** Foreclosure is a structural property of representation, and representation is necessary for computation. A classifier that did not foreclose anything would not classify. The architectural question is therefore not "how do we eliminate foreclosure" but "how do we measure it, bound it, and preserve information that allows future re-interpretation when the current foreclosure is recognized."

2. **The architectural alternative must work within physical resource constraints.** A 40 MHz input stream at the LHC cannot be preserved in full; the architectural question is what to preserve and what to forfeit, given a hard bandwidth ceiling. "Preserve everything" is not an architectural alternative; it is a refusal of the question.

3. **The architectural alternative must support open-world output.** The most mechanically specific architectural requirement is that the classifier output space must include a category for *I do not know what this is, and I cannot project it onto the known* — Mechanism VIII's negation. This is non-trivial. It requires a model of ignorance, not just a model of normality.

4. **The architectural alternative must support cross-representation disagreement preservation.** Multiple representations of the same event, with the disagreement among them preserved as a separate signal, addresses Mechanisms II and V directly. Protocol III of the operative paper instantiates this for the LHC; the general principle applies elsewhere.

5. **The architectural alternative must support temporal invariance under detector / model evolution.** Anchor samples preserved in their original form across successive trigger and reconstruction generations, with periodic per-generation replay. Protocol II of the operative paper instantiates this.

6. **The architectural alternative must publish what it foreclosed.** The institutional commitment to retention maps — per-stage accounting of what each gate makes unrecoverable — is itself an architectural feature, not a documentation feature.

These are five necessary conditions on any answer. They do not constitute the answer. The Assembly turn should be directed toward sketching specific architectural alternatives that meet (some subset of) these conditions and demonstrate (some subset of) the foreclosure-mitigation capabilities under realistic resource budgets.

Candidate architectures that should be considered include:

- **Ensemble of representation-distinct anomaly detectors with disagreement-preservation.** Multiple autoencoders with structurally distinct input representations (object-level, calorimeter-image, detector-channel) feeding a meta-classifier that preserves events on disagreement, regardless of any individual anomaly score.
- **Open-world classifiers with explicit "unknown" output.** Architectures (energy-based models, distance-aware deep learning, evidential deep learning) that can output bounded confidence and explicit non-coverage rather than projecting onto the known.
- **Distillation-resistant architectures.** Architectures that do not require teacher-student compression for hardware deployment, or that perform compression in a way that preserves teacher decisions about edge cases (Mechanism IV mitigation).
- **Reconstruction-free anomaly detection.** Architectures that do not rely on reconstruction error as a novelty signal (Mechanism II mitigation), such as density estimation in a learned feature space, or contrastive methods that detect distributional shift directly.
- **Generative-augmentation for unknown-unknowns.** Generative models trained to produce events that are systematically *outside* the training distribution, used as adversarial validation signals. This is closest to what would constitute a "validation against the unknown-unknown" — by construction, not by hope.
- **Constitutional retention.** Architectural commitments to preserve specific event populations (cross-representation-disagreement events, calibration-shift events, low-multiplicity events, displaced-vertex events, late-timing events, etc.) regardless of classifier score, on the grounds that these populations are systematically vulnerable to the eight mechanisms.

The Assembly turn should consider these candidates (plus others the substrates surface) and produce drafts that:
- Specify the architectural alternative in enough detail to be reviewable.
- Identify which mechanisms it addresses and which it does not.
- Estimate its resource cost relative to current architectures.
- Identify what would constitute evidence that it is operating as intended.

The output of the Assembly turn would be a third document, drafted as a follow-up architectural proposal, sibling to the operative paper and this deposit.
